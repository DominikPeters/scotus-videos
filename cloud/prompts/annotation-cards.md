Editorial lessons from Suncor (25-170):
- Neutrality: supply necessary background or show the exact material under discussion. Never act as a referee by introducing outside material that supports one side in a live disagreement. Ask: is this exact source being discussed here? If not, is it neutral background or evidence bearing on the disagreement? For example, do not answer Barrett’s disputed damages-versus-regulation question with a lower court’s unmentioned characterization of the relief. Likewise, a lower court’s description of its own “original jurisdiction” must not appear to settle a disputed finality question. Balanced comparisons of the parties’ positions are welcome when the exchange frames that choice.
- Show what is cited. If counsel names paragraph 411, show paragraph 411; if Kavanaugh cites Ouellette footnote 20, fetch that footnote rather than a similar main-text passage. Official U.S. Reports PDFs on govinfo include footnotes and syllabi that other text repositories omit.
- Timelines may explain a sequence of precedents even when the dispute is legal, not chronological. Kavanaugh’s Milwaukee I / Milwaukee II / Ouellette / AEP sequence is an excellent example. A case’s procedural stage and recent agency actions can also merit timelines. Maximum five events, normally about five words per event label. Inspect at full-frame video size.
- State a factual source’s status plainly (for example “Proposed rule”) without framing the card as correcting a Justice.
- Use descriptive headings naming the source or concept, such as “Displacement vs. preemption” or “American Electric Power Co. v. Connecticut (2011)”; avoid teaser questions and vague editorial slogans. Scope holdings precisely: AEP concerned power-plant greenhouse-gas emissions, not all CO₂ emissions.
- For Record images, cards_exhibit quote selects the whole contiguous paragraph; optional highlight selects only the key phrase within it. Crop tightly, retaining the full passage width with minimal top/bottom whitespace. Small type is a reason to improve the crop before abandoning the original record.
- Check each evidence quote against its claimed physical page. Check displayed statutory/excerpt text against that source too. Inspect the complete scene, not just the card crop. Keep omission decisions as readable private notes. Save Luna’s scan incrementally.
- Review long gaps across the complete argument for missed references, but do not pad gaps that need no explanation. The Suncor set benefited from the added jurisdiction question, Atlantic Richfield, Cox, Watson, the four-precedent timeline, the compared saving clauses and Isla Petroleum. These are worked editorial examples, not a checklist for other cases.
