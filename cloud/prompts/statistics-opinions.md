Publication contract (supersedes any instruction above to omit a decided case):
- decision.type is a CLOSED opinion-form enum. Use majority for an ordinary Court opinion, even when unanimous or accompanied by concurrences. Code derives the public 9-0 / 6-3 etc. label from judgment votes. Never put prose, unanimity, vote counts or partial-join explanations in type.
- Use plurality opinion only where the principal opinion actually lacks a Court majority on the controlling reasoning. Separate opinions or a partial concurrence do not by themselves make a plurality.
- voteStatus=complete means one supported case-level judgment coalition. Keep judgment votes distinct from full reasoning joins. A concurrence in judgment counts toward the prevailing judgment; a partial dissent is not automatically a minority vote on the whole case.
- If different claims have different judgment coalitions, use voteStatus=mixed, empty case-level voter/joiner/writer arrays, null sizes, and judgmentParts with a short claim label, its exact coalition/counts and supporting passage for EACH part. Keep the known principal author. Do not suppress the decision or invent one overall vote. Cisco (ATS 6-3; TVPA 8-1) is an example.
- If a disposition is known but an exhaustive coalition is not, use voteStatus=unknown with empty vote arrays, null sizes, empty judgmentParts and the known form (e.g. dismissed as improvidently granted or per curiam). A decided case must not disappear because named votes are unavailable. decision=null is for pending cases only.
- opinion_details.joiners means full joiners of THAT opinion, excluding the author. Put partial joiners in partialJoiners=[{justice,parts}]. The parts field describes the opinion's scope. It must not be the only place partial joins are recorded. A justice joining only Part II is not a full joiner.

SCDB taxonomy guidance and worked comparators:
- A statute without its own named entry is not missing evidence. Code 600, Infrequently litigated statutes, covers supported statutory grounds without a more specific entry: MGM v Grokster (copyright), Global-Tech v SEB (35 USC 271(b)), Stinson/Mistretta/Booker (Sentencing Guidelines). Do not substitute a constitutional clause just because its subject matches a statute.
- Code 900, No Legal Provision, is appropriate when the actual ground is nonstatutory common law; null means insufficient evidence, not simply no specifically named statute. Explain the chosen basis privately.
- Franchise Tax Board v Hyatt (2018) is SCDB issue 80060 / provision 224 for interstate state sovereign immunity, even in state court. This is a taxonomy convention, not a claim that every such suit is literally in federal court.
- Peugh v US: federal ex post facto sentencing issue 10390 / provision 122. Do not choose issue 10180 labelled ex post facto (state) for federal punishment merely because its leaf wording sounds specific.
- Granfinanciera: civil Seventh Amendment jury issue 10580 / provision 218. Some SCDB parent categories sound surprising; use the precedent and actual issue rather than renaming the taxonomy.
- Compare the holding with the taxonomy's full label, including parenthetical qualifications. A source-supported classification may still be wrong if it describes a different procedural issue. Keep uncertainty private and do not invent codes.
