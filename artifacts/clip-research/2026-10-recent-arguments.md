- **Hook:** consent, or stop selling personal information. Clear stakes and an unusually sharp ending.
- **Exchange:** Kagan to Paul Clement; Kavanaugh raises consent just before her, and Clement responds just afterward. Suggested 25–45 seconds including the response, rather than presenting it as an unanswered pronouncement.
- **Locator:** Kagan's turn **52:11.52–52:31.68**. Search `Take your pick` or `stop selling`. Verified in local aligned transcript `artifacts/vertical-cloud-test/bundle/json/2026-10-14-25-459-argument-1-audio.json`.
- **Coverage:** [SCOTUSblog, October 14](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/10/court-appears-likely-to-allow-internet-suit-based-on-decades-old-video-privacy-law-to-go-forward/) singles out the two alternatives. Direct article fetch failed, but its [indexed Reddit reproduction](https://www.reddit.com/r/UsaNewsLive/comments/1x61chg/court_appears_likely_to_allow_internet_suit_based/) exposes the relevant article text.
- **Context:** whether Salazar is a protected consumer under the VPPA; do not imply the Court has resolved liability or that every tracking practice is illegal.

### 2. Beaird: Gorsuch's binding commentary / advisory guidelines paradox

- **Hook:** `topsy-turvy world`—an accessible tension in sentencing rules that needs only a brief setup.
- **Exchange:** Gorsuch to Eric Feigin; retain Feigin's answer. Aim for roughly 40–70 seconds, depending on where the answer resolves.
- **Locator:** official transcript **printed page 57, lines 18–20**, continuing onto page 58. A second version occurs at pages 104–105 with Anthony Dick. No verified audio timestamp in this research pass.
- **Coverage:** [Balls & Strikes, Madiba K. Dennie, October 13](https://ballsandstrikes.org/scotus/beaird-v-united-states-neil-gorsuch-sentencing/) prominently discusses this tension. This is opinionated commentary; the passage is useful independently of its partisan framing.
- **Primary source:** [current official transcript](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2026/25-5343_d1oe.pdf). The previously indexed `_m6io.pdf` URL now returns 404, so use the current index/link.
- **Context:** guidelines are advisory after Booker, but courts must calculate them correctly; avoid suggesting commentary automatically dictates the final sentence. The later Gorsuch–Dick version may be the cleaner complete exchange.

- **Locator:** **27:10–28:59** is the surrounding discussion; the punchline starts **27:59** and response **28:13**. A shorter candidate is roughly **27:35–28:48**. Local timed `annotation-trial/25-170/transcript.txt` verifies these locators.

- **Context:** Kagan challenges the constitutional-structure theory, while separately considering statutory preemption. Do not frame this as her rejecting every possible argument against Boulder's lawsuit.

### 4. Salazar: Roberts brings back Breyer's pet snail

- **Hook:** a snail in your pocket and a park's no-pets sign—a compact, memorable way to introduce statutory purpose versus literal wording.
- **Exchange:** Roberts to Joshua Hammack. The question itself runs **3:05.46–3:37.20**; include Hammack's answer for a roughly 45–65 second clip.
- **Locator:** `pet snail` in the same local aligned Salazar transcript.
- **Coverage:** [SCOTUSblog, October 14](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/10/court-appears-likely-to-allow-internet-suit-based-on-decades-old-video-privacy-law-to-go-forward/) picks out this hypothetical; article text available through the reproduction linked above.

### 5. Anderson: what counts as an apple?

- **Hook:** comparing retirement investments becomes an apples-and-oranges debate. Works especially well as an approachable explanation of why raw returns do not settle whether a fund was prudently managed.
- **Exchange:** Thomas asks Matthew Wessler about apples versus oranges; Wessler asks what counts as either. Kagan later expands the analogy, allowing differences in color/texture/taste while insisting the comparator still be an apple. Prefer one continuous exchange; do not splice distant turns as if consecutive.
- **Locator:** Kagan begins **7:48**, Wessler responds **9:00**, according to [the timed argument transcript](https://supremecourt.report/arguments/2026/25-498.html). These are secondary alignment timestamps and need checking against our pipeline before rendering. Search `kind of an apple`; her main turn is approximately 72 seconds, so the earlier Thomas exchange may make a tighter clip.
- **Coverage:** [Your ERISA Watch, Peter Sessions, October 7](https://www.yourerisawatch.com/2026/10/your-erisa-watch-week-of-october-7-2026/) expressly calls the fruit exchanges amusing and quotes Thomas–Wessler. [SCOTUSblog's October 7 report](https://www.reddit.com/r/UsaNewsLive/comments/1wzydq2/justices_emphasize_comparing_apples_to_apples_in/) foregrounds the same analogy. [NAPA's postargument report](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2026/10/justices-probe-meaningful-benchmark-standard-in-intel-arguments/) also discusses Kagan's criticism.
- **Context:** pleading a retirement-plan prudence claim; avoid turning it into generic financial advice or suggesting lower returns alone prove misconduct.

- **Locator:** `cap emissions in the other 49 states`, then `same thing can happen through the tort suit`. Verified in local timed transcript.

## What this suggests for future clip discovery

Search postargument reports first, extract the specific exchanges attracting attention, and then match those phrases to our own aligned transcript. Rank complete question–answer units, not isolated one-liners. Preserve research source links and confidence separately from clip captions. A coverage match is a promising lead rather than a requirement: specialist reporting can reveal very good accessible clips even when a case has little general-news attention.
